Security and responsible disclosure

Last updated 24 August 2026. What we do, and how to tell us when we got it wrong.

What an audit touches

  • Public DNS records for the domain you submit: MX, TXT, and CNAME lookups.
  • For MTA-STS and BIMI, an HTTPS fetch of a policy or asset the domain owner published deliberately at a well-known location.
  • Nothing else. No port scanning, no SMTP connection, no mail delivery.

Controls in the service

  • Optional API keys, required on every /v1 route once configured.
  • Sliding-window rate limiting per key, falling back to per-IP when unauthenticated.
  • Bounded outbound HTTP timeouts, so a slow third-party policy file cannot pin a worker.
  • A content security policy on every HTML page, plus nosniff, X-Frame-Options: DENY, a strict referrer policy, and HSTS over HTTPS.
  • An X-Request-Id on every response so you can correlate a report with our logs.

Reporting a vulnerability

Email hello@inboxready.dev with "security" in the subject. Include what you found, how to reproduce it, and the X-Request-Id if a specific response is involved.

  • We acknowledge within one business day.
  • We will tell you our assessment and a fix timeline within five business days.
  • We will credit you when the fix ships, unless you would rather we did not.
  • We will not pursue you for good-faith research that respects the rules below.

Rules for testing

  • Test against your own domains and your own account.
  • No denial of service, no traffic floods, no social engineering of the team.
  • Stop at proof of concept — do not pivot, persist, or exfiltrate.
  • Give us a reasonable window before publishing.

Self-hosting checklist

  • Set INBOXREADY_API_KEYS and keep INBOXREADY_RATE_LIMIT_PER_MINUTE non-zero.
  • Set INBOXREADY_ENVIRONMENT=production so crawlers are allowed and staging copies are not.
  • Set INBOXREADY_SITE_BASE_URL so canonical URLs and the sitemap point at the real origin.
  • Terminate TLS in front of the app; HSTS is emitted only on HTTPS requests.
